Decision Brief · Customer Satisfaction

Counting the reviews

A customer who leaves us a five-star Google review does not count toward our customer satisfaction score. Only people who answer the GatherUp survey do. That is about a third of everyone telling us how we did, and we have been grading branches without them.

Starting Q3, we measure both as one number: the Customer Satisfaction Index. NPS stays on the scorecard as a reference.

How this came up

We were looking at Fort Worth. The branch had very little survey data behind its score, and in the 9/17 huddle Mike pushed back that the number was not real. So we went and checked whether the five-star Google reviews Fort Worth was earning counted toward its NPS.

They did not. None of them.

That is the gap. And it gets worse the better a branch is at asking, because the two asks compete: if a manager encourages a customer to leave a Google review, that customer very often does that instead of the survey. We are effectively coaching people into the channel we do not count. A branch can be doing everything right and watch its score sit still, or drop.

Which is why NPS on its own is not a fair way to judge true customer satisfaction at a branch. It is still a good reference number and it stays on the scorecard. It is just not the whole picture, and we have been treating it as though it were.

This is not a new idea

The 8/28 NPS ruling already found the real problem: response rate is the volatility lever — N. Austin was converting 8.7% of requests while Houston converted 53.1%. That ruling left exactly one question open for the Q3 QGR packet: our Green band is 3 points wide and the noise in the number is about ±7 points.

CSI is the answer to that question. It grows the sample instead of widening the band.

The objection, tested

Why these two channels can be added together

The serious objection to blending is that survey responses and online reviews are different populations answering different questions. We tested it against our own Q3 data. They are not.

91.5% promoter
Reviewers we never counted (n=283)

Four points apart on the promoter share. Same customers, same moment, same ask — we simply give them two places to put the answer.

The "five-star scales have no middle" objection dies on our own data

The textbook complaint about counting reviews is that a 0–10 scale has a real passive band and a 5-star scale does not. Ours does not either: 507 of our 603 survey responses this quarter are literally 10s. Einstein customers give a 10 or they tell us something is wrong. A five-star and a 10 are behaving as the same signal.

And it cannot be gamed the way you would expect

  • The GatherUp link goes out automatically to every customer and a branch cannot suppress it. The unhappy-customer channel is always open.
  • A branch can only add reviews, never subtract survey responses. Adding real five-star customers who would otherwise stay silent is the behavior we want.
  • The dual ask — leave a review here, give management feedback here — is part of the design system. A branch that skips it collects less CSI, not more. The incentive is self-correcting.
  • GatherUp exists so an unhappy customer can reach management instead of posting publicly. That is deliberate and it stays.

Honest limits

What this does not fix

CSI measures satisfaction × surfacing effort

Two branches with identical service and different review hustle will post different CSI. A branch can raise CSI by asking more people, not only by getting better. We consider asking part of the job — it drives SEO and it is already a Games metric — so the distortion points at behavior we want. Guardrail: watch reviews-per-move alongside CSI. If CSI climbs and complaint volume does not fall, we surfaced signal rather than improved.

We moved the distortion, we did not remove it

NPS rewarded survey-chasing. CSI rewards review-chasing. The second is the better behavior, but neither is pure satisfaction.

The band-width question from 8/28 is still open

A 47% bigger denominator cuts the noise from roughly ±7 points to about ±6, against a Green band 3 points wide. Better, not solved. This still belongs at the Q3 QGR packet.

Every number on this page is a ceiling

The analysis matched duplicate customers on name. Google display names are often a first name and an initial, or all lowercase, and will not match the full name in GatherUp, so we are almost certainly finding fewer duplicates than exist, which pushes CSI slightly high. The build spec fixes this; until it ships, read these figures as the optimistic end.

Build spec · Matisen

What it takes to ship

Where

The hub, alongside the existing nps_* metrics. Today NPS comes from tools/nps_pull.py (GatherUp API v2) and review volume comes from the Games engine — two spines with no join. CSI needs the join.

Emit csi_monthly, csi_quarter, csi_trailing_90d, csi_week at branch / region / company grain, matching the existing nps_* shape so dashboards and scorecards read it without special-casing. Every nps_* metric keeps emitting unchanged.

Inputs — both already available

  • Survey responses — NPS Report export, /{businessId}/dashboard/customer-level-reporting/generate. Uses first/last name, email, NPS Rating (0-10), Received, Job ID, Review Site Clicked.
  • Reviews — Reviews Report export, /dashboard/report/success/review-data-csv?businessId=…. Uses name, rating, reviewDate, type (google/yelp), email.
  • GatherUp exposes API Credentials under the account menu — prefer the API over scraping these endpoints.

Algorithm

  1. Window survey responses by Received; window reviews by reviewDate.
  2. Categorize — survey 9–10 promoter, 7–8 passive, 0–6 detractor; review 5★ promoter, 4★ passive, ≤3★ detractor.
  3. Dedupe to one record per customer. On conflict, latest wins.
  4. CSI = (promoters − detractors) / total × 100, one decimal.
  5. Suppress the graded stamp when total < 50.

The dedupe is the real work — and the numbers on this page move if it tightens

The analysis matched on normalized full name. Google display names will not match GatherUp's full names, so the current figures under-count duplicates and therefore overstate CSI. Required: match on email or Job ID first where present (exact, auto-accept), fall back to fuzzy name within a move-date window, and send everything below the confidence threshold to a human review queue at end of quarter, before the branch is stamped — these are confirmed, these are fuzzy, someone eyeball them. The quarter-end pass is the control; we are not trying to build a perfect algorithm.

Out of scope: anything about how NPS itself is collected or calculated. NPS keeps emitting exactly as it does now.
